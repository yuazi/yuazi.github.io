> [!abstract] Course Summary
> Data Science for Business Data (Dr. Henning Baars, Information Systems I) covers discriminative AI on business data from an application perspective: when data science fits a process, which methods apply, how models get built, operationalized and distributed, and what the organization needs. The exercises use Python on data from a water management case.

## Lecture Notes

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/01-aim-and-structure|(y-01) Aim and Structure]]  
   Course contents, the place of the course in the WI1 curriculum next to Applied AI, discriminative versus generative AI, the course objective, the teaching approach and the key questions.

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/02-relevance-and-context|(y-02) Relevance and Context]]  
   AI and ML adoption in German enterprises, AI's contribution to innovation, where companies use ML, barriers to adoption, the method zoo, the pace of research and real-world cases from warranty cost forecasts to Uber and predictive maintenance for freight cars.

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/03-central-concepts|(y-03) Central Concepts]]  
   Data science, business intelligence and analytics, advanced analytics, model-driven DSS, reporting and OLAP, descriptive, predictive and prescriptive analytics, data mining and ML, Cyc and the limits of hand-coded rules, learning paradigms, AI textbooks over time, strong and weak AI, big data and a conceptual map of all terms.

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/04-system-context|(y-04) System Context]]  
   New system landscapes, the model and its tools, tool types for the model lifecycle and foundation models, the data scientist's tool environment, fraud detection, process mining and data science environments, AutoML and no-code AI, programming languages and Python, distributing results and models, workbenches and cloud analytics.

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/05a-organizational-context|(y-05a) Organizational Context]]  
   Organizational challenges, data quality dimensions, data governance, owners and stewards, the BICC, embedding data science roles in a matrix, data scientists versus data analysts, KDD, SEMMA, CRISP-DM and DataOps, data exploration and distributions, correlation, data wrangling, dummy attributes, standardization and overfitting.

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/06-outlier-analysis|(y-06) Outlier Analysis]]  
   Outlier detection and generative AI: monitoring prompts, RAG pipelines and agent actions for anomalies and drift, using language models to explain outliers, and classical methods as the baseline for tabular data. Covers slide 12 only until the full deck is posted.

- [[/notes/lectures/datasciencebusinessdata/07-classification|(y-07) Classification]]  
   The confusion matrix, accuracy, recall, precision, specificity, F-score and MCC, ROC curves and AUC, k-NN, classification trees, impurity and pruning, random forests, bagging and boosting, SVMs with soft margins and kernels, logistic regression and odds, and naive Bayes with a spam example.
