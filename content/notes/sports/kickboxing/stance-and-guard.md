The kickboxing stance is a compromise between three demands. Punching works best side-on, the way boxers stand. Kicking needs the hips free to turn. Defending low kicks needs you to be able to lift the lead leg at any moment without shifting your weight first. The result is a stance more square than boxing, and much more square than a taekwondo sparring stance.

---

## (y-) Mental model

Stand so you could lift either foot off the floor right now without leaning. If you have to shift your weight before you can lift the lead leg, you're too heavy on it, and a low kick will land before you can check it. Most of the details below come back to that test.

## (y-) The stance, step by step

Orthodox stance (left foot forward). Southpaws mirror everything.

1. **Feet about shoulder width apart.** Step the left foot forward so the heel of the front foot is roughly in line with the toes of the back foot. Your feet shouldn't be on one line like a tightrope walker. Leave a gap sideways between them, like standing on two railway tracks.
2. **Point the toes.** Lead foot pointing roughly forward or slightly turned in. Rear foot angled out about 45 degrees.
3. **Lift the rear heel slightly.** Weight on the ball of the back foot, so you can push off it for a cross or a rear kick.
4. **Soften the knees.** Bent enough to drop into a punch or lift into a check, but not crouched.
5. **Center your weight.** Close to 50/50 between the feet, or a little more on the back foot. The weight-forward boxing stance leaves the lead leg planted under you, and it's easy to kick.
6. **Turn the hips only slightly.** Less side-on than boxing, so the rear leg can swing through and the lead leg can lift straight up.

## (y-) The guard

1. **Rear glove at the cheek or jaw.** Close enough to touch your face. This hand blocks hooks and head kicks on that side.
2. **Lead glove a little in front, at eye level.** It measures distance, parries jabs, and starts most combinations.
3. **Elbows down and in.** They cover the ribs and the liver from body kicks and hooks.
4. **Chin down, shoulders slightly up.** You should look at your opponent through your eyebrows.
5. **Eyes on the chest.** Watching the chest and shoulders lets you see the hands and the hips in your peripheral vision, and the hips start every kick.

The high guard is the default because head kicks are legal. A low, boxing-style guard with the lead hand by the hip invites a rear head kick.

## (y-) Footwork

- **Step and drag.** Move the foot closest to where you're going first, then bring the other one the same distance. Forward means lead foot first. Back means rear foot first. Never cross your feet.
- **Small steps.** A long step leaves you on one leg for a moment, which is exactly when a low kick lands.
- **Pivot.** Turn on the ball of the lead foot, swinging the rear foot around, to step off the line of an attack and face them from a new angle.
- **Step out.** A short step diagonally forward with the lead foot opens the hips for a rear kick. It's the first movement of the [[notes/sports/kickboxing/kicks|(y-) roundhouse]].
- **Switch.** A small hop that swaps your lead foot. Used to load the lead-leg switch kick or change stance against a southpaw.

## (y-) Open and closed stance

When both fighters lead with the same foot, you're in a closed stance. When one is orthodox and the other southpaw, it's open. The idea is the same as in [[notes/sports/taekwondo/sparring|(y-) taekwondo sparring]]: in open stance, your rear kick goes to their open side, the body and liver are exposed to the rear roundhouse, and both lead feet fight for the outside position. Against a southpaw, try to keep your lead foot outside theirs so your cross lines up and their cross doesn't.

## (y-) Drills

| Drill              | How                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lift test          | In stance, lift the lead foot, then the rear foot, without moving your upper body. If you have to lean, adjust the stance. |
| Mirror rounds      | Three minutes of stance and guard in a mirror. Check chin, elbows and rear hand every time you move.                       |
| Box steps          | Step forward, right, back, left in a square, always in stance, never crossing the feet.                                    |
| Partner tag        | Partner reaches to tap your shoulder or thigh. You only move your feet to stay out of reach.                               |
| Shadow with checks | Shadowbox for a round, and every few seconds lift the lead leg into a [[notes/sports/kickboxing/low-kicks\|(y-) check]].   |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                         | Fix                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Feet on one line                | Step the rear foot out to the side. Balance comes from the sideways gap.                                    |
| Too much weight on the lead leg | Sit back toward 50/50 until you can lift the lead foot instantly.                                           |
| Stance too wide                 | A wide stance is stable for punching but slow to check and slow to kick. Narrow it.                         |
| Rear hand drifting off the face | Touch the glove to the cheek after every combination until it stays there on its own.                       |
| Chin up when tired              | Shoulders up, chin down, especially in the last minute of a round.                                          |
| Big bouncing                    | A light rhythm is fine. Bouncing high off both feet is a taekwondo habit that makes low kicks easy to land. |
