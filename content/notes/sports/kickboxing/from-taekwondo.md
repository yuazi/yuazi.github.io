Taekwondo gives you a head start in kickboxing: kicking speed, hip mobility, balance on one leg, and a feel for distance. It also leaves you with habits built for a different rulebook. In WT sparring nobody punches you in the head, nobody kicks your legs, and a kick only has to press hard enough to trip a sensor. Kickboxing takes all three of those away at once.

---

## (y-) Mental model

The kicks are the same, but the target map is different. The [[notes/sports/taekwondo/rules|(y-) WT rules]] protect everything below the belt and the whole head from punches, so the stance, guard and footwork are built around that. Kickboxing makes the legs a target and the head a target for fists, and scores damage instead of sensor hits. Most of the changes below follow from those two facts.

## (y-) What carries over

| From taekwondo               | Why it helps                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hip mobility and kick height | Head kicks are legal and scored highly. Most new kickboxers struggle to get the leg up at all. |
| Distance reading             | Knowing exactly where the edge of your kicking range is, and staying there.                    |
| Single-leg balance           | Every kick, check and knee happens on one leg.                                                 |
| Front-leg speed              | The [[notes/sports/taekwondo/cut-kick\|(y-) cut kick]] reflex turns into a fast lead teep.     |
| Spinning kicks               | Legal under Glory rules, and spinning techniques get priority when judges count clean strikes. |
| Counter-kicking              | Taekwondo sparring is full of counters. The timing transfers directly.                         |

## (y-) What to change

| Taekwondo habit            | What happens in kickboxing                                                                         | What to do instead                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bladed, side-on stance     | The lead thigh is wide open to low kicks, and you can't throw the rear hand.                       | Square up. See [[notes/sports/kickboxing/stance-and-guard\|(y-) Stance & Guard]].               |
| Hands low by the belt      | You get punched in the face.                                                                       | High guard, all the time, gloves on the cheeks.                                                 |
| Bouncing high on both feet | Low kicks land as your feet touch down, and you can't punch from mid-bounce.                       | A small rhythm with the heels just off the floor.                                               |
| Kicking with the instep    | An instep that meets an elbow or a checking shin gets hurt.                                        | Kick with the shin and turn the hip all the way over.                                           |
| Snap and retract           | Glory only counts strikes thrown with full power, and judges rank damage above the number of hits. | Swing through the target.                                                                       |
| Leaning back from punches  | Leaning back takes you out of range of one punch and into the next.                                | Block, slip a little, and counter.                                                              |
| Never checking             | Low kicks were never a threat, so the reflex doesn't exist.                                        | Drill [[notes/sports/kickboxing/low-kicks\|(y-) checks]] in every session for the first months. |

## (y-) The first three months

1. **Hands every session.** The punches are the biggest gap. Do a round of [[notes/sports/kickboxing/punches|(y-) jab, cross and hook]] work every time, even on kicking days.
2. **Check drills.** Ten minutes of partner check-and-counter work, until lifting the leg happens without you thinking about it.
3. **Shin kicks on the heavy bag.** Light at first. The shins need weeks to get used to impact, and the hip turn needs repetitions before it replaces the taekwondo snap.
4. **Keep stretching.** The [[notes/sports/taekwondo/stretching|(y-) flexibility work]] is the biggest advantage taekwondo gives you, and it fades if you stop.
5. **Spar light.** Expect to get hit with punches more than you're used to. Light sparring lets you build the guard without paying for every mistake.

## (y-) Using both at once

Training both at the same time works if you keep the two contexts separate. The kicks are close enough that each sport feeds the other. The stance, guard and kicking surface are different enough that you need to switch modes consciously when you change rooms. Mixing them up is most likely in the first few minutes of a session, so spend the warm-up in the stance for that sport.
