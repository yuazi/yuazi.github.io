The low kick is the roundhouse thrown at the thigh. It's the signature weapon of Dutch kickboxing: cheap to throw, hard to avoid, and after a few clean ones the other fighter can't put weight on that leg. Ernesto Hoost won four K-1 World Grand Prix titles with combinations that almost always finished on one.

Checking is the defense, and it gets taught together with the kick because nobody learns one without being on the receiving end of the other. World Taekwondo rules don't allow kicks below the waist, so this whole page was new territory for me.

---

## (y-) Mental model

The kick is a short, heavy swing of the shin through the meat of the thigh, angled slightly downward. The check puts your shin in the way so their shin hits bone instead of muscle. Both are about the hips. The kick needs the hip turned all the way over to have any weight behind it. The check needs your weight centered enough that you can lift the leg in time.

## (y-) Outside low kick

Orthodox stance, right leg into the outside of their lead (left) thigh.

1. **Step out a little.** A shorter step than for the body kick, because the target is closer.
2. **Pivot on the lead foot.** Heel turns toward the target.
3. **Turn the hip over and swing down through the thigh.** Aim at the side of the thigh, about halfway between hip and knee, and cut through at a slight downward angle.
4. **Land with the lower shin.** Not the foot, and not the knee.
5. **Keep the hands up.** You're close enough for them to punch you while you're on one leg. The left glove stays at your face. The right arm can swing, but less than on a body kick.
6. **Return to stance or follow up.** Their weight shifts after a good low kick, which is a good time to throw a cross.

Aim for the thigh, not the knee joint. A kick that lands on the knee hurts you as much as them and is how training partners get injured.

## (y-) Inside low kick

A kick to the inside of their lead thigh, thrown with your lead or rear leg. It's lighter, it's faster, and it disrupts their stance more than it damages them. In a closed stance (both orthodox), the inside of their lead leg faces your lead leg, so a quick lead-leg kick there is short and hard to see coming.

## (y-) Calf kick

The low kick aimed lower, at the calf below the knee, and from slightly closer. It needs less wind-up and lands on a part of the leg that's harder to protect, so it's more difficult to check. Thrown well, it takes the leg out from under them fast. Thrown lazily, it ends up as shin-on-shin.

## (y-) Checking, step by step

You're orthodox. Their right leg is coming at your left thigh.

1. **Lift the lead leg.** Knee up, foot off the floor, no weight shift first. If you have to lean back before you can lift, your [[notes/sports/kickboxing/stance-and-guard|(y-) stance]] is too front-heavy.
2. **Turn the knee out toward the kick.** The shin turns to face the incoming leg, so their shin meets your shin and the bone near the knee.
3. **Bring the same-side elbow down toward the knee.** It closes the gap to the body. The gloves stay high.
4. **Put the foot down and counter.** Right after the check, they're on one leg and possibly in pain. A cross or your own low kick lands well here.

Against calf kicks, lift earlier and turn the knee out more, or step the lead foot back out of range.

## (y-) Other defenses

- **Pull the leg back.** Step the lead foot back out of range so the kick whiffs. It works, but if you do it every time they'll kick the rear leg instead.
- **Punch on the kick.** Throw a straight punch the moment they lift the leg. They're on one foot and can't move their head much.
- **Change angle.** Step off the line so their hip can't turn into the kick properly.

## (y-) Shin conditioning

Shins get used to impact from regular bag work over months. Start with light kicks and add power over weeks. If a shin gets a deep bruise, give it time before kicking hard again, and keep working the other leg in the meantime.

## (y-) Drills

| Drill             | How                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Thigh-pad kicks   | Partner holds a Thai pad vertically against the outside of their thigh. Light kicks, then full ones.                |
| Low bag           | Heavy bag rounds aiming at the bottom third, cutting downward through it.                                           |
| Check on tap      | Partner taps your thigh lightly with their shin, at random. Check every one, then counter.                          |
| 1-2-low kick      | The classic. On pads, then bag, then shadowboxing. See [[notes/sports/kickboxing/combinations\|(y-) Combinations]]. |
| Check and counter | Partner throws a light low kick, you check and immediately throw a cross to the pad.                                |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Kicking with the foot                      | Turn the hip further over so the shin arrives first.                                       |
| No hip turn                                | Without the turn, it's a leg swing. Pivot and drive the hip through.                       |
| Kicking straight across                    | Angle slightly downward through the thigh.                                                 |
| Hands dropping on the low kick             | You're in punching range. Keep the guard.                                                  |
| Lifting the knee straight forward to check | Turn the knee out to meet the kick. A straight-up knee leaves the thigh exposed beside it. |
| Checking and standing still                | Every check is followed by a counter.                                                      |
