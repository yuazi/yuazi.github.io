The kicking in kickboxing comes mostly from Muay Thai by way of Dutch and Japanese gyms. The rear roundhouse is thrown with the shin and swung through the target with the whole hip behind it. It's slower to retract than a taekwondo kick, and much heavier when it lands. Under K-1 and Glory rules, knees are legal too, which is why they share this page.

## (y-) Mental model

Think of a baseball bat instead of a whip. The taekwondo roundhouse chambers the knee and snaps the lower leg out and back. The kickboxing roundhouse keeps the leg long, turns the hips over completely, and swings through as if the target were a few centimeters further back. The shin is the bat. The foot only matters when the distance is long and the foot is what reaches.

## (y-) Rear roundhouse, step by step

Orthodox stance, kicking with the right leg to the body.

1. **Step out.** Take a short step diagonally forward and to the left with the lead foot, toes turned out. This opens the hips and gets your head off the center line.
2. **Pivot on the ball of the lead foot.** As the kick comes, the heel of the support foot turns toward the target. For a head kick, you'll need to turn further.
3. **Swing from the hip.** Drive the right hip forward and over. The leg rises nearly straight, slightly bent at the knee, with no chamber.
4. **Land with the lower shin.** On the body, aim at the ribs under the elbow or the liver side. The shin lands, then the hips keep turning through.
5. **Use the arms.** The right arm swings down and back for counterbalance and power. The left glove stays at your face. Kickboxers often keep the swinging arm higher than Muay Thai fighters, because there's a punch waiting on that side.
6. **Return to stance.** If the kick lands, bring it back the way it came. If it misses, let the turn carry you around and land back in stance rather than stopping half-turned with your back exposed.

## (y-) Head kick

The same kick with more pivot and more hip. It needs real hip mobility, which is where the [[notes/sports/taekwondo/stretching|(y-) taekwondo stretching]] and [[notes/sports/taekwondo/splits-progression|(y-) splits work]] pay off. Throw it after you've made them think about something else: a body kick that brings their elbows down, a low kick that drops their attention, or a punch combination that ends with their hands in the wrong place. A head kick thrown cold from a standing start is easy to see and easy to block.

## (y-) Teep (push kick)

1. Lift the knee straight up in front of you.
2. Push the ball of the foot (or the whole sole) into their belly or hip, extending the hip forward.
3. Lean the upper body back slightly as a counterweight, hands up.
4. Retract and land in stance.

The teep is a jab with the foot. It stops someone walking forward, knocks them off balance as they step in, and keeps a pressure fighter at the end of your range. Thrown with the lead leg it's fast. With the rear leg it's stronger but takes longer.

Under Glory rules, linear kicks (front kicks, side kicks, back kicks) aren't allowed to the legs. A teep to the thigh is a foul there, so keep it on the body and head.

## (y-) Switch kick

A lead-leg roundhouse with a small hop first. Orthodox stance:

1. Hop and switch the feet in place, so the left foot lands behind and the right foot in front.
2. Kick straight away with the left leg, now the rear leg, using the same mechanics as the rear roundhouse.

The switch gives the lead leg time to build power. Done well, it's fast enough to land on the body or open side before they react. It also fits naturally after a cross, because the cross leaves your left hip pulled back and ready to swing.

## (y-) Spinning kicks

Spinning back kicks, spinning hook kicks, and spinning back fists (with the back of the glove) are all legal under Glory rules, and Glory's scoring gives spinning and flying techniques priority when counting clean strikes. The mechanics carry over directly from the [[notes/sports/taekwondo/spinning-back-kick|(y-) taekwondo spinning back kick]]. The risk is different, because you turn your back at a range where your opponent can punch. Throw them as counters when they step in, and rarely as openers.

## (y-) Knees

- **Straight knee.** From the rear leg, drive the hip forward and the knee up into the body or into a face that's ducking. Rise up on the ball of the support foot.
- **Knee from the clinch.** Under Glory rules, you can grab only to throw one knee immediately, and then you have to let go. Holding for any other reason is a foul. Muay Thai lets fighters stay locked in the clinch trading knees and trying sweeps; under Glory rules it's one knee and out.
- **Jumping knee.** Legal and spectacular. Use it against someone who covers up and walks forward.

## (y-) Drills

| Stage | Drill                                                                                                                        |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | **Pivot and turn.** Without a bag, step out, pivot, turn the hip over, and swing the leg slowly. Ten each side.              |
| 2     | **Bag at half power.** Body-height roundhouses, focusing on landing with the shin and turning all the way through.           |
| 3     | **Thai pads.** Partner holds two pads together at body height and meets the kick. Rounds of single kicks, then doubles.      |
| 4     | **Teep the advance.** Partner walks forward with a pad on the belly. Teep each time they step.                               |
| 5     | **Punch to kick.** 1-2 then switch kick, or 1-2-3 then rear kick. The punches load the kick.                                 |
| 6     | **Head-kick ladder.** Body, then shoulder, then head height on the pads, only as high as you can go with the hip fully over. |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                         | Fix                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kicking with the instep         | Land with the shin. An instep that meets an elbow is how people break small bones in the foot.    |
| Snapping and stopping           | Swing through the target. Aim a few centimeters behind it.                                        |
| Support heel stays planted      | Pivot. The hip can't turn over if the support foot doesn't turn.                                  |
| Both hands drop during the kick | The non-kicking hand stays on your face. One arm swings, one guards.                              |
| Leaning back too far            | You need some lean to counter the leg, but leaning far back means you can't punch after the kick. |
| Throwing a head kick cold       | Set it up with punches or body kicks first.                                                       |
