Six punches, each with a number. Coaches call combinations by number on the pads ("one, two, three, low kick"), so learning the numbers is part of learning the punches. Most gyms use the numbering below, though some swap the two uppercuts, so ask on day one.

| Number | Punch         | Hand |
| ------ | ------------- | ---- |
| 1      | Jab           | Lead |
| 2      | Cross         | Rear |
| 3      | Lead hook     | Lead |
| 4      | Rear hook     | Rear |
| 5      | Lead uppercut | Lead |
| 6      | Rear uppercut | Rear |

---

## (y-) Mental model

Every punch is a turn of the hips, and every turn of the hips loads the next strike in the opposite direction. The cross turns you one way, so the lead hook comes back the other way. The lead hook turns you back again, so the rear leg is now wound up for a kick. That's why so many kickboxing combinations end with "three, rear low kick": the hook loads the kick. The [[notes/sports/kickboxing/combinations|(y-) combinations]] page builds on this.

A kickboxing detail that boxing doesn't have to worry about: wherever your weight lands after a punch, that leg can be kicked. Throw the cross, but don't fall onto your lead leg behind it.

## (y-) Jab (1)

1. Push off the rear foot and take a small step with the lead foot if you need the distance.
2. Extend the lead hand straight from the guard, no wind-up.
3. Turn the fist over at the end so the palm faces down and the knuckles land.
4. Lift the lead shoulder to cover your chin. The rear glove stays on your cheek.
5. Bring the hand back along the same line, faster than it went out.

The jab measures distance, blinds the opponent for a moment, and starts almost every combination. A double jab moves you forward without committing anything else.

## (y-) Cross (2)

1. Pivot on the ball of the rear foot so the heel turns outward.
2. Turn the rear hip and shoulder forward together. The hand travels straight along the center line.
3. Turn the fist over at the end, palm down.
4. Pull the lead hand back to the face as the cross goes out.
5. Return to stance straight away. Your weight shifts forward during the punch and comes back after it.

## (y-) Lead hook (3)

1. Shift a little weight onto the lead foot and pivot on it, heel turning out.
2. Lift the elbow to the height of the fist, arm bent at roughly 90 degrees.
3. Turn the hips and shoulders through together. The arm doesn't swing on its own, it rides along with the turn.
4. Land with the palm facing down or facing you. Coaches teach both. Pick one and keep the wrist straight.
5. Keep the rear glove glued to your face. Your opponent's rear hook is coming from that side.

To the body, bend the knees to lower your level instead of leaning over. A lead hook to the liver (on their right side if they're orthodox) is one of the best body shots there is.

## (y-) Rear hook (4)

Same mechanics from the rear side, with more travel and more power. It's slower and easier to see coming, so it works best after a lead hook or cross has turned them, or as a counter when they lean in.

## (y-) Uppercuts (5 and 6)

1. Drop your level slightly by bending the knee on the punching side. Don't drop the hand to load it.
2. Drive up with the legs and turn the hip.
3. The fist travels up with the palm facing you, elbow bent.
4. Hit under the chin or into the solar plexus, then return to guard.

Uppercuts are for close range, against someone who leans forward or covers up high. From outside range they're slow and leave the face open.

## (y-) Drills

| Drill               | How                                                                                                        |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Jab-only round      | A whole round of nothing but jabs on the bag or in shadowboxing: single, double, to the body, stepping in. |
| Numbers out loud    | Shadowbox while saying the numbers. It trains the vocabulary and the movement together.                    |
| Return to the face  | After every punch, touch the glove to your cheek before the next. Slow, then fast.                         |
| Called combinations | Partner or coach calls numbers on the pads. React to the call, not to a memorised sequence.                |
| Punch and check     | Throw a 1-2, then lift the lead leg into a check. Trains you not to fall onto the lead leg.                |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                              | Fix                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pulling the hand back before the jab | Throw straight from the guard. Any cocking motion is a signal.                                 |
| Flat rear heel on the cross          | Pivot. The power comes from the rear hip turning, and the hip can't turn past a planted heel.  |
| Rear hand dropping during the hook   | Glue it to the cheek. Hooks are traded, and theirs comes to the side your hand just left.      |
| Wide, looping hooks                  | Elbow up, short arc, turn the body. A hook that starts wide is seen from the moment it leaves. |
| Leaning over the lead leg            | Stay over your base. Leaning puts your weight where a low kick can take it away.               |
| Chin up after punching               | Shoulder up on the jab, chin down on everything.                                               |
