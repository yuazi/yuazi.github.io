Most kickboxing training happens in three-minute rounds with a minute's rest, the same shape as a pro fight under Glory rules. Amateur WAKO ring bouts use two-minute rounds, so if you're training for one of those, set the timer to two. Either way, train in rounds. A long session without breaks never teaches you to recover in sixty seconds and go again.

## (y-) Mental model

The three tools do different jobs:

| Tool         | What it trains                                                          | What it can't do                                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shadowboxing | Technique, footwork and defense together, at whatever speed you choose. | No impact, so it can't tell you if a strike has weight behind it. |
| Heavy bag    | Power, shin conditioning, and keeping your form while tired.            | It doesn't move or hit back.                                      |
| Pads         | Timing, distance and reactions, because a person is moving them.        | Only as good as the person holding them.                          |

A good session uses all three, in that order: shadowbox to warm up the technique, pads while you're fresh enough to be sharp, bag when you're tired enough to need the discipline.

## (y-) Shadowboxing

- Stay in stance and move your feet the whole round. If your feet stop, it's just waving your arms in the air.
- Work on one idea per round: jab only, low kicks and checks, defense and counters, or one combination with different exits.
- Imagine an opponent in front of you. Throw at a target height and distance, and defend things they would throw back.
- Check your guard in a mirror when you can. Hands drop in shadowboxing more than anywhere else, because nothing punishes it.

## (y-) Heavy bag

- Move around it. Circle, step in, step out, and avoid standing still to hit it from one spot.
- Kick the lower third for low kicks and the middle for body kicks. Land with the shin and turn the hip through.
- When the bag swings back, treat it as a counter coming at you and keep the guard up.
- Build power gradually. Your shins and hands need weeks to get used to a hard bag. Start at half power.
- Keep working through the last minute of each round. That is where you learn to throw the same combination when tired.

## (y-) Pads

Thai pads (the long rectangular ones, worn on the forearms) are for kicks and knees. Focus mitts are for punches. Many gyms use both, with the holder switching between them.

### Holding Thai pads for a body kick

1. Hold both pads together vertically on the side being kicked, at body height.
2. Elbows bent, pads slightly away from your body.
3. Meet the kick. Push the pads into it just as it lands, so the impact doesn't travel through your arms into your ribs.
4. Brace the leg on that side and turn slightly into the kick.

### Holding for a low kick

Hold one pad vertically against the outside of your thigh, with your hand on the top edge. Turn your leg slightly so the pad faces the kick. Don't hold it out in the air, because a low kick swings down through the target.

### Being a good holder

- Call the combination clearly and early.
- Move between combinations, so the striker has to find range.
- Throw a light jab or swing a pad back at them now and then to remind them to keep their guard up.
- Watch their form and say something if it slips.

## (y-) Sample session (about 60 minutes)

| Block        | Time        | What                                                                                                                            |
| ------------ | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Warm-up      | 10 min      | Skipping rope, joint circles, light dynamic stretches. See the [[notes/sports/taekwondo/warmup-routine\|(y-) warm-up routine]]. |
| Shadowboxing | 3 × 3 min   | Round 1 stance and footwork, round 2 punches, round 3 kicks and checks.                                                         |
| Pads         | 4 × 3 min   | Called combinations from the [[notes/sports/kickboxing/combinations\|(y-) combinations]] page, swap holders after each round.   |
| Heavy bag    | 3 × 3 min   | Round 1 low kicks, round 2 combinations, round 3 hard work on the final minute.                                                 |
| Conditioning | 8 min       | Burpees, squat jumps, core work. See [[notes/sports/taekwondo/conditioning\|(y-) conditioning]] for round-shaped intervals.     |
| Cool-down    | 5 to 10 min | Easy stretching, especially hips and calves.                                                                                    |

One minute rest between all rounds.

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                              | Fix                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hitting the bag with no defense      | After every combination, cover, check or step out as if the bag was hitting back.                                                   |
| Arm punching when tired              | Fewer punches with the hips turning beat many punches from the shoulders.                                                           |
| Kicking a hard bag with the instep   | Shin, hip turned over. The instep is for the head, if anywhere.                                                                     |
| Loose wraps                          | Re-wrap. A loose wrap on a heavy bag is how wrists get sprained. See [[notes/sports/kickboxing/hand-wrapping\|(y-) Hand Wrapping]]. |
| Stiff pad holding with locked elbows | Bend the elbows and meet the strike. Locked arms send every kick into your shoulders.                                               |
| Hands dropping in the last minute    | Touch the gloves to your face after every combination, and do it most in the last minute.                                           |
