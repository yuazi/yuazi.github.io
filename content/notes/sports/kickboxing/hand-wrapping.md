Wraps go on before the gloves in every session where you hit something. They hold the wrist straight, keep the small bones in the back of the hand from shifting against each other, and stop the knuckles rubbing raw inside the glove. The glove pads the punch, and the wrap holds the hand together underneath it.

The order of the thumb and finger passes differs between tutorials. Pick one and follow it all the way through, because mixing two patterns is how you end up with a wrap that's loose in one place and strangling you in another.

## (y-) Mental model

Every pass of the wrap does one of three things: it locks the wrist straight, it binds the knuckles into one solid unit, or it adds a bit of padding over them. The wrist matters most. A sprained wrist from a hook that folded on the bag takes longer to heal than skinned knuckles. If you lose track halfway through, work out which of the three the current pass is for.

## (y-) Choosing wraps

Buy 180-inch wraps (about 4.5 m). That's the standard adult length and leaves enough cloth for the wrist, the knuckles and the finger weaves. The 120-inch ones are for kids and small hands.

Mexican-style wraps are a cotton blend with some stretch, so they mold to the hand. Traditional wraps are plain woven cotton with no give, which means you set the tension yourself on every pass.

Quick wraps and gel inner gloves go on faster but hold the wrist less well. They're a fine backup for a bag session when you're short on time, but learn the real wrap first. Glory bans inner gloves in fights altogether.

## (y-) Step by step

Right hand shown. Mirror everything for the left.

1. **Thumb through the loop.** Back of the hand facing up, fingers spread wide. The roll should come off over the back of your hand, not across the palm.
2. **Wrist, three or four times.** Wrap around the wrist and the base of the hand, covering the joint. Keep the wrist straight and the tension snug.
3. **Knuckles, three times.** Go up diagonally across the back of the hand and around the knuckles three times, fingers still spread.
4. **Back to the wrist.** Come down diagonally across the back of the hand and go once around the wrist.
5. **Weave between the fingers.** From the wrist, go up across the back of the hand, through the gap between the pinky and ring finger, and back down to the wrist. Go around the wrist, then repeat through the ring and middle finger gap, then the middle and index gap.
6. **Lock the thumb.** Loop once or twice around the base of the thumb and come back to the wrist. Tight enough to support it, not so tight it throbs.
7. **Use up the rest.** Cross the back of the hand in an X a few times, or add one or two more passes over the knuckles if you want more padding there.
8. **Finish on the wrist.** Close the Velcro on the wrist, never on the back of the hand where it digs in under the glove.

## (y-) Checking the wrap

- Make a tight fist. It should close all the way and feel firm. Throbbing, tingling, or pale fingertips mean it's too tight, so unwrap and start again.
- Open the hand and spread the fingers. The knuckle layer should lie flat with no ridge of bunched cloth.
- Try to bend the wrist. It should feel stiff, which is what you want.
- Put the glove on. The glove's cuff should sit over the wrapped wrist, so the two support each other.

## (y-) Fight wraps vs training wraps

In competition, hands are wrapped with soft bandage and tape while an official watches. Under Glory's 2026 rules, tape over the knuckles can be at most 1 mm thick (three strips), building up tape to pad them is banned, and an inspector has to examine and stamp the wraps before the gloves go on. Cloth training wraps do the same job, can be reused, and are what you'll wear in the gym.

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                                | Fix                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wrapping with a closed fist            | Spread the fingers while wrapping. A wrap done on a fist cuts in once the hand opens and starts hitting. |
| Too tight                              | Pale or tingling fingertips. Unwrap and redo it with less tension on the wrist and knuckle passes.       |
| Too loose at the wrist                 | The wrist folds on hooks. Three or four snug passes before you go anywhere near the knuckles.            |
| Twisted cloth                          | Keep the wrap flat on every pass. A twist becomes a hard ridge under the glove.                          |
| Velcro closing on the back of the hand | Plan the last passes so the end lands on the wrist.                                                      |
| Yesterday's damp wraps                 | Wash and fully dry them between sessions, and wrap clean, dry hands.                                     |

## (y-) Looking after them

Wash wraps after every session or two, in a mesh laundry bag so they don't knot themselves around everything else, and let them air-dry. Roll them starting from the Velcro end with the Velcro folded in, so the thumb loop is on the outside and ready the next time you wrap.
