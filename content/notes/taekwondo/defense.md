Most of the sparring notes on this site are about scoring. This one is about the other half: not giving up points while you look for yours. Under electronic scoring a head kick is worth three, a spinning head kick five, and a round ends early at a 15-point gap, so a few clean kicks conceded in a row can end a round.

Defense in WT sparring comes in four layers, and the outer ones are cheaper than the inner ones: distance, movement, the guard, and the counter.

---

## (y-) Mental model

Every attack has to cross a distance, come from an angle, and get past your arms. You can stop it at any of those three points. The further out you stop it, the less it costs you. Blocking with the arms works, but it is the last layer, and an arm block still leaves you standing in range.

## (y-) Layer 1: distance

Stand just outside the reach of the opponent's longest kick, usually their back-leg roundhouse. From there they have to step to reach you, and the step is what you react to. The [[notes/taekwondo/footwork|(y-) Footwork]] page breaks the space into distance bands and covers how to move between them.

A half step back is often enough. The opponent's kick lands in the air, and they are left on one leg in front of you.

## (y-) Layer 2: movement

| Movement           | How                                                                                         | Sets up                                                 |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Step back          | Rear foot moves first, front foot follows. Stay in stance.                                  | A back-leg roundhouse or a push kick as they land.      |
| Switch             | Change lead foot in place, which turns your closed stance into an open one.                 | Taking away the target they were aiming at.             |
| Pivot off the line | Turn on the front foot so your body rotates out of the kick's path.                         | A counter from the side while they are still committed. |
| Cut                | Lift the front leg into their step. See [[notes/taekwondo/cut-kick\|(y-) Cut & Push Kick]]. | Your own kick off the lifted leg.                       |

Going backward in a straight line works a few times, then you meet the boundary. Since 2026 any part of the foot over the line is a gam-jeom, so a defense that only retreats gives points away on its own.

## (y-) Layer 3: the guard

The sparring guard keeps both hands up in front of the body, elbows close to the ribs. The rear hand protects the face and head on the open side, the front hand moves to meet body kicks. Fighters adjust it to their style, but a few things hold for everyone.

- Keep the rear hand high. Most head kicks arrive on the side the rear hand covers.
- Block with the forearm, not the hand. An open hand reaching for a kick gets the fingers hurt and often misses.
- Turn the body with the block. A forearm block with the trunk square still lets a kick slip past it to the hogu.
- Bring the hands back up after every kick you throw. The moment right after your own attack is when you are easiest to hit.

## (y-) Layer 4: the counter

A defense that ends without a counter gives the opponent a free try. Each defensive move above has a natural counter attached to it.

| Opponent's attack       | Defense                  | Counter                                         |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Back-leg roundhouse     | Half step back           | Back-leg roundhouse as their foot lands         |
| Rushing forward         | Pivot off the line       | Spinning back kick into the side they left open |
| Front-leg head kick     | Rear-arm block, turn     | Body punch to stop the follow-up, then kick     |
| Double roundhouse       | Cut the second kick      | Front-leg roundhouse off the lifted leg         |
| Clinch and inside kicks | Hands tight, stay square | Short hook kick or push to break the clinch     |

The spinning back kick is the classic counter to forward pressure because it travels straight back along the line the attacker is coming down. The [[notes/taekwondo/spinning-back-kick|(y-) Spinning Back Kick]] page has the full breakdown.

## (y-) Drills

| Drill             | How                                                                                                         |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Distance tag      | Partner tries to touch your hogu with a slow roundhouse. You may only step back or switch, no blocking.     |
| Arm-only round    | Feet stay planted. Partner throws slow kicks to body and head, you block with the forearms and turn.        |
| Defend and answer | Every defensive move must be followed by a kick within one second, or it does not count.                    |
| Boundary escape   | Start one step from the line. Partner pressures you. Escape by pivoting out, not by stepping back.          |
| Hands-up check    | During pad rounds, the holder taps at your head after every kick you throw. Catch them with a raised guard. |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                           | Fix                                                                             |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Retreating in a straight line     | Mix in pivots and switches before you run out of mat.                           |
| Turning the back to avoid a kick  | This is a penalty and leaves the back of the head open. Pivot or block instead. |
| Hands drop after kicking          | Drill the hands-up check until the guard returns on its own.                    |
| Reaching for kicks with the hands | Block with the forearms close to the body.                                      |
| Blocking without countering       | Use the defend-and-answer drill. Every block should lead to a kick.             |
