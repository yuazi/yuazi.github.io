Footwork (bal-gisul) gets you to the right distance on your terms and out again before the other person can answer. It decides a lot before any kick is thrown: a perfect roundhouse from the wrong distance hits air, and a sloppy one from the right distance still scores two points.

For the tactics that sit on top of this, see [[notes/taekwondo/sparring|(y-) Sparring]].

---

## (y-) Mental Model

You are a spring that is always slightly loaded. Weight on the balls of the feet, knees soft, a small rhythm going so you never have to start moving from a dead stop. When you decide to go, the energy is already in your legs.

Distance comes in three bands:

| Band   | What fits there                                                                                     |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Long   | Out of reach for both of you. Safe, but nobody scores. You need a step to get in.                   |
| Middle | Front-leg kicks land without a step. Most scoring happens here, and so does most getting scored on. |
| Close  | Chest to chest. Punches, short roundhouses, hook kicks around the back, clinch work.                |

Good footwork is mostly about moving between these bands faster than your opponent can adjust.

---

## (y-) The Core Steps

### Bounce (Rhythm Step)

A small, light hop in place on the balls of the feet. It keeps the legs loaded and hides the moment you start an attack, because you are already moving. Vary the rhythm. A metronome bounce is easy to time a counter against.

### Step Forward and Back

The front foot moves first going forward, the back foot moves first going back. Keep the distance between your feet the same throughout so you never end up in a long, stretched stance that you cannot kick from.

### Slide (Shuffle)

Both feet move together in a short skip, forward or back, without crossing. It covers ground fast and keeps you in stance. Most front-leg attacks start with a slide forward.

### Switch

Swap your lead leg in place with a small jump. Switching changes which side of the hogu is open to you and which leg is closest to the target. It also resets an opponent who has started to time your rhythm.

### Cut Step (Drag Step)

The rear foot slides up to the front foot, then the front leg kicks. It closes distance while keeping the front leg ready, and it is the usual setup for a front-leg roundhouse or axe kick.

### Pivot and Angle Out

Step off the straight line instead of straight back. A quarter turn on the front foot takes you out of the path of a straight attack and leaves the opponent kicking where you used to be, with you standing beside them.

## (y-) Using the Boundary

The mat is an 8m octagon, and stepping over the line costs a gam-jeom. Under the 2026 rules any part of the foot crossing the line counts, unless the opponent's prohibited conduct pushed you out (see [[notes/taekwondo/rules|(y-) Rules]]). So the line works against whoever is closer to it.

- Cut off the ring. Step to the side your opponent wants to escape toward, so the only way out is straight back.
- Know where the line is behind you without looking. If you need to check, you are already too close.
- When your back is near the edge, angle out along the line instead of retreating into it.

## (y-) Drills

| Drill                | How                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mirror               | Partner leads, you match every step and switch at the same distance. Swap roles every 30 seconds.                     |
| Rhythm break         | Bounce steadily for eight counts, then attack on an off beat. Partner tries to call the moment you go.                |
| Ladder or tape lines | Fast feet through an agility ladder or taped squares. Short sets, full speed, balls of the feet only.                 |
| Distance tag         | Partner holds a pad at chest height and moves around. Score only by kicking it the moment it comes into middle range. |
| Boundary pressure    | One fighter starts near the line and works to escape along it. The other cuts off the angles without kicking.         |
| Two-minute rounds    | Shadow footwork for a full round length (two minutes), one-minute rest, three rounds, same as a real match.           |

## (y-) Common Mistakes

| Mistake                       | Fix                                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Flat feet                     | Stay on the balls of the feet. Heels can touch, but the weight stays forward. |
| Crossing the feet             | Lead with the foot closest to where you are going.                            |
| Retreating in a straight line | Angle out. A straight retreat just gives a pressure fighter more chances.     |
| Bouncing too high             | Keep the bounce small. Height wastes energy and slows the first step.         |
| Same rhythm all round         | Change speed, pause, double the beat. The other person is timing you.         |
