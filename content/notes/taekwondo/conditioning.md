A WT match is best of three rounds, each two minutes long, with a minute's rest between them. Inside each round the work comes in bursts: a few seconds of kicking, a few seconds of bouncing and reading, then another burst. Conditioning for it means being able to repeat explosive kicks with short recoveries, and still having a high kick left in the last ten seconds of round three.

Long, slow runs help a little with recovery between rounds but do little for the bursts, so the sessions below are built around repeated short efforts.

---

## (y-) Mental model

Train in the shape of a round: two minutes of mixed effort, one minute of rest, three times over. If your training never looks like that, the first real match will feel much harder than any session did.

There are three things to build, and they are trained differently:

| Quality               | What it does in a match                                   | How to train it                              |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Power                 | Fast first step, fast kick, jump spins.                   | Short plyometric sets with full rest.        |
| Repeat-sprint ability | Throwing the fifth combination as fast as the first.      | Intervals shaped like rounds.                |
| Single-leg strength   | Balance on the support leg, higher kicks, safer landings. | Slow, heavy-enough unilateral strength work. |

## (y-) Power: plyometrics

Do these early in a session while you are fresh. Quality drops fast with fatigue, and a tired jump teaches a slow jump.

| Exercise         | Sets × reps    | Note                                                                     |
| ---------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pogo hops        | 3 × 20         | Stiff ankles, short ground contact. This is the sparring bounce, loaded. |
| Tuck jumps       | 3 × 6          | Knees to chest, land soft and quiet.                                     |
| Lateral bounds   | 3 × 6 per side | Jump sideways off one leg, stick the landing for a second.               |
| Split jumps      | 3 × 8          | Switch legs in the air, as in a switch step.                             |
| Jump spin (180°) | 3 × 5 per side | Jump, turn half a turn, land in stance. Trains the spin entry.           |

Rest at least a minute between sets. The aim is maximum speed on every rep.

## (y-) Repeat sprints: round intervals

The core session. Use a timer set to two minutes on, one minute off.

| Round | Work (2 minutes)                                                                         |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Bag or pads: 5 seconds of all-out kicking, 10 seconds of bouncing, repeat for the round. |
| 2     | Same pattern, with a spin kick in every burst.                                           |
| 3     | Same pattern, and the final 20 seconds are continuous kicking.                           |

Rest a minute between rounds, then rest three to five minutes and repeat the block once or twice. That gives two or three simulated matches. When it gets easy, shorten the bouncing to seven seconds.

The last ten seconds matter more since the 2026 rules made a passivity gam-jeom in that window worth two points. Train yourself to finish rounds attacking.

## (y-) Single-leg strength

Twice a week, on days without hard interval work if possible.

| Exercise                     | Sets × reps     | Why                                                           |
| ---------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bulgarian split squat        | 3 × 8 per leg   | Front-leg strength and hip flexor length on the back leg.     |
| Single-leg Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8 per leg   | Hamstrings and balance on the support leg.                    |
| Side-lying leg raises        | 2 × 15 per leg  | Hip abductors, for side kick height.                          |
| Calf raises (single-leg)     | 3 × 15 per leg  | The bounce lives in the calves.                               |
| Pallof press                 | 3 × 10 per side | Anti-rotation for the trunk, which keeps spins on their axis. |

The [[notes/fencing/workout|(y-) Fencing Workout]] page has more no-equipment options for the core and posterior chain. The same exercises work for both sports.

## (y-) A sample week

| Day       | Session                                   |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Monday    | Technique class, then plyometrics (short) |
| Tuesday   | Single-leg strength                       |
| Wednesday | Sparring class                            |
| Thursday  | Round intervals                           |
| Friday    | Technique class, flexibility              |
| Saturday  | Single-leg strength, easy 20-minute jog   |
| Sunday    | Rest                                      |

Keep the hardest sessions away from the day before sparring. Kicking tired is how technique slips and how hamstrings get pulled.

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                             | Fix                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Only running long distances         | Keep one easy run a week at most. Use round intervals for match fitness.    |
| Plyometrics at the end of a session | Move them to the start, when you can still jump fast.                       |
| Same intensity every day            | Alternate hard and easy days.                                               |
| Skipping single-leg work            | Every kick is thrown on one leg. Train the support leg on purpose.          |
| No warm-up before intervals         | Use the [[notes/taekwondo/warmup-routine\|(y-) Flexibility Warm-Up]] first. |

> [!warning] Load
> Add volume slowly. Jumping and kicking are hard on the knees and Achilles tendons. If a joint hurts the next morning, cut the plyometric volume in half for a week.
