Left-handed fencers are far more common at the top of épée than in the general population. The [[notes/fencing/stances|(y-) Stances]] page puts it at about 30 percent of elite épéeists against roughly 10 percent of people overall. The usual explanation is practice volume. A right-hander might see a lefty a few times a month, while a lefty fences right-handers almost every bout. When the two meet, the lefty is on familiar ground and the righty is running habits that were built for a mirror image.

This page is written from the right-hander's side. If you are a lefty, read it backwards and remember that another lefty is the opponent you get the least practice against.

---

## (y-) The Geometry

In a righty against righty bout, the two fencers' chests face opposite sides of the strip. Your inside line (the chest side, quarte) lines up with their outside line (the back side, sixte), and the blades cross over each other.

Against a lefty, both chests face the same side of the strip and both backs face the other. Your inside line lines up with their inside line, outside with outside. Both weapon arms are on the same side, so the blades sit side by side instead of crossing.

| What changes             | Righty vs righty                               | Righty vs lefty                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Lines facing each other  | Your inside vs their outside                   | Inside vs inside, outside vs outside                |
| Natural blade engagement | Your sixte against their sixte, blades crossed | Both in sixte on the same side, blades side by side |
| Their back and flank     | On the side of your inside line                | On the side of your outside line                    |
| Their chest              | On the side of your outside line               | On the side of your inside line, same as your chest |

In practice, a lot of the automatic reactions you trained against righties now point the wrong way. A disengage you have done a thousand times can go straight into the line the lefty is already covering, and a parry that closed the line against a righty can end up closing a line nobody is attacking.

---

## (y-) What to Do About It

### Rebuild the Defaults

Decide before the bout which parry is your main one against a lefty, and practice it on purpose. If your reflex parry is four, check where a lefty's straight attack lands before you trust it.

### Work the Outside

With both outside lines facing each other, the lefty's outside forearm and flank sit on the side your blade already occupies in sixte. Short angulated hits to the top and outside of the wrist from sixte are worth drilling specifically for this matchup.

### Watch the Wrist from the Other Side

Everything on the [[notes/fencing/tactics|(y-) Tactics]] page about stop-hits to the wrist still applies, but the wrist is presented at a different angle. Spend the first touches of the bout watching where their hand sits before you commit to anything.

### Get Reps

What works best is fencing more lefties. If your club has one or two, ask them for practice bouts. If not, a right-handed partner can fence left-handed for drills. They will be slow, but the lines and angles will be correct, and that is what you need to retrain.

---

## (y-) Drills

| Drill             | How                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mirror engagement | Partner fences left-handed. Engage in sixte, take turns beating and disengaging slowly until the new direction feels normal. |
| Parry check       | Lefty partner attacks straight at a set speed. Name the parry before you make it, then make it.                              |
| Outside wrist     | From sixte engagement, angulate to the top and outside of the wrist. Ten hits, rest, repeat.                                 |
| Lefty-only bouts  | Short bouts to five touches against left-handers only. Note which of your usual actions failed.                              |

---

## (y-) Common Mistakes

| Mistake                             | Fix                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Running the righty game plan        | Spend the first touch or two observing. Adjust before committing.         |
| Disengaging into their covered line | Check which way your disengage goes against a lefty and drill it.         |
| Avoiding lefties in practice        | Ask them for bouts. You learn the matchup by fencing it.                  |
| Treating every lefty the same       | They differ from each other as much as right-handers do. Read the person. |
