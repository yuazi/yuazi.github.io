A fencing competition is a long day with short bursts of effort. You might fence six pool bouts of three minutes each over two hours, wait an hour, then fence direct elimination bouts until you lose. Most of the day is waiting, which is where food, staying warm, and warm-up timing come in.

---

## (y-) How the day runs

| Stage              | What happens                                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Check-in           | Confirm you are fencing. Late check-in can mean you are scratched from the event.                |
| Pools              | Groups of usually six or seven fencers. Everyone fences everyone else to 5 touches in 3 minutes. |
| Seeding            | Results from the pools rank the fencers for the direct elimination table.                        |
| Direct elimination | Bouts to 15 touches, three periods of 3 minutes. Win and you advance, lose and you are out.      |

Some competitions cut a share of the field after the pools, so the bottom fencers do not reach the DE table. Check the format in the event information before the day.

## (y-) How seeding works

After pools, fencers are ranked by these criteria, in order:

1. Victory ratio: bouts won divided by bouts fenced.
2. Indicator: touches scored minus touches received.
3. Touches scored.

The indicator is why every touch in a pool counts, even in a bout you are losing. A 5-4 loss and a 5-0 loss both count as one defeat, but they differ by four on the indicator, and that can change your seed by several places. A better seed usually means an easier first DE.

## (y-) What to pack

| Item                                    | Note                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two working épées, at least             | Test both before you leave. A third is better.                          |
| Two body cords                          | Épée needs body cords only. No lamé or mask cord.                       |
| Mask, jacket, plastron, breeches, glove | Check they meet the event's rules (FIE-approved kit at higher levels).  |
| Socks, shoes, chest protector           | Long socks that cover the leg to the breeches.                          |
| Test weight and shim                    | To check your tip pressure (750 g) and travel before weapon control.    |
| Spare tips, screws, small screwdriver   | Tips fail more often than blades.                                       |
| Food and water                          | Small, easy snacks. Bananas, rice cakes, sandwiches.                    |
| Warm clothes                            | Venues are often cold, and you will sit for a long time between rounds. |

The [[notes/fencing/equipment|(y-) Equipment]] page explains the tip pressure and travel rules in detail.

## (y-) Warm-up and timing

Start warming up about 30 to 40 minutes before pools. Use the [[notes/fencing/stretching|(y-) Fencing Stretching]] routine, then footwork, then a few minutes of light bouting if a partner is free. Between pools and the DE table there is often a long gap. Do a shorter warm-up again about 15 minutes before your first DE, because an hour of sitting cancels the first one.

## (y-) Between bouts

- Write two lines after each pool bout: what scored for you, what scored against you. The [[notes/fencing/bout-analysis|(y-) Bout Analysis]] page has a template.
- Watch your next opponents in the pool while you wait. Note their favorite action and their distance.
- Eat small amounts often. A large meal between pools and DEs makes you slow.
- Keep moving lightly. A short walk and a few lunges keep the legs ready.

## (y-) In the DE

A 15-touch bout is long enough to make a plan and change it. Use the first few touches to test the opponent's reactions, and use the one-minute breaks to decide on a single change. The rules on doubles, the priority minute, and non-combativity are on the [[notes/fencing/score-and-clock|(y-) Doubles, Priority & the Clock]] page. Know them before the bout, because there is no time to work them out at 13-13.

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                               | Fix                                          |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| One weapon, one body cord             | Bring at least two of each, tested.          |
| Giving up touches in a lost pool bout | Fight for every touch. The indicator counts. |
| Warm-up only once                     | Warm up again before the DE table.           |
| Not eating                            | Plan food for a whole day.                   |
| Changing everything at the break      | Pick one change and try it.                  |
