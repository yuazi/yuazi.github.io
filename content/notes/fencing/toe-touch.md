Épée is the only weapon where the foot is valid target, and the front foot is the closest part of the body to your point after the hand and wrist. The toe touch drops the point onto the top of that foot, usually as the opponent steps forward. When it works it is one of the cleanest touches in the sport. When it fails, your head and arm are low and open, which is why people get hit on the mask trying it.

The [[notes/fencing/tactics|(y-) Tactics]] page lists it among the épée-specific patterns. Here is the technique in full.

---

## (y-) Mental model

The toe touch is a timing hit on a target that moves twice: it lifts and it lands. You aim for the moment the foot lands, because a foot in the air is moving fast and a foot on the floor is still. The point drops from your normal line, so the action is short: the hand lowers and the knees bend a little.

## (y-) Step by step

1. Start at a distance where the opponent's front foot is just inside the reach of your extended arm plus a small lunge. This is usually a bit closer than you would stand to attack the wrist.
2. Watch the front foot, with the blade and wrist in your peripheral vision.
3. As the opponent advances, extend the arm and drop the point toward where their front foot will land, not where it is now.
4. Bend the knees and lower the hips as the arm drops, so the upper body stays upright. Keep the guard turned to cover the inside line.
5. Hit the top of the foot with the point and keep the pressure on long enough to register (the tip needs 750 grams).
6. Recover backward immediately, raising the point back to your line.

## (y-) Technique notes

- Lower the body with the legs. Bending at the waist drops your head toward the opponent's point, and that is how most mask counters land.
- Keep the guard high and angled on the way down. It is the only cover your upper arm has while the point is low.
- Time it to the step. Against a fencer standing still, a toe touch is a long, slow action, and they have time to hit you on the top of the head.
- Know the 40 ms rule. If they counter to the mask and both lights come on, it is a double, as the [[notes/fencing/equipment|(y-) Equipment]] page explains. That can be fine when you are ahead and bad when you are behind.

## (y-) When it works

| Situation                                         | Why the toe is open                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Opponent advances with a big front step           | The foot lands in front of their guard and stays there for a moment.                                  |
| Opponent holds the hand high to protect the wrist | Their point is high, so their counter to your head is slower.                                         |
| Opponent attacks your wrist from far out          | Their lunge brings the front foot close. A stop-hit to the toe is shorter than a stop-hit to the arm. |
| Late in a period when the opponent pushes forward | They take bigger steps and watch your blade, not the distance.                                        |

## (y-) When it does not

Against a fencer who keeps their point low and in line, dropping your point walks your mask onto theirs. It also fails against short, quick steps, where the foot never sits still long enough. And it becomes predictable: after one toe touch, a good opponent will advance and then stop, waiting for your point to drop so they can hit you on the head.

## (y-) Defending the toe

The best defense is a short front step and a point that stays low enough to threaten the opponent's head if they drop. Some fencers pull the front foot back as the opponent's point drops, which is a small retreat with the front foot only, and then counter to the mask or the arm.

## (y-) Drills

| Drill               | How                                                                                                              |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Target on the floor | Tape a coin-sized mark on the floor at lunge distance. Drop the point onto it from en garde. Then from a lunge.  |
| Partner steps       | Partner advances slowly. Hit the toe as the foot lands. Vary the step length so you have to read the distance.   |
| Toe or counter      | Partner advances. If they keep the point high, hit the toe. If they drop it, hit the wrist instead.              |
| Head check          | Partner tries to touch your mask lightly as you drop to the toe. Keep the upper body upright and the guard high. |
| Feint step          | Partner advances and sometimes stops halfway. You must not drop until the foot lands.                            |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                      | Fix                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bending at the waist         | Lower the hips and keep the back upright.                |
| Going for a stationary foot  | Wait for the step. Time it to the landing.               |
| Point drops, guard drops too | Keep the guard high and angled to cover the inside line. |
| No recovery                  | Retreat immediately after the hit, hit or miss.          |
| Trying it every exchange     | Use it once or twice per bout, so it stays a surprise.   |
