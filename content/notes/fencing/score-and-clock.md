In épée the scoreboard and the clock change what a good action is. A double touch helps the leader at 12-10 and is annulled at 14-14. A minute of careful fencing with no touches now puts a penalty touch on the board for both fencers. Below are the rules that decide the end of a bout and how they change the choices you make.

The 40 ms window that creates doubles is explained on the [[notes/fencing/equipment|(y-) Equipment]] page.

## (y-) Bout format

| Bout                    | Touches | Time                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pool                    | 5       | 3 minutes                                                      |
| Direct elimination (DE) | 15      | Three periods of 3 minutes, with a 1-minute break between them |

The bout ends when one fencer reaches the touch limit or the time runs out. If time runs out with one fencer ahead, that fencer wins.

## (y-) Doubles

When both fencers land within the lockout, both lights go on and each fencer scores a touch. That has a simple consequence: a double keeps the gap the same and moves both fencers closer to the end.

| Score situation                          | What a double does                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| You lead by one or more                  | Helps you. The gap stays and the bout gets shorter.                           |
| You trail                                | Hurts you. You need single touches, and each double uses up touches and time. |
| One touch from the end, you lead (14-13) | Wins the bout. 15-14 is a finished bout.                                      |
| Tied at the last touch (4-4, 14-14)      | Does nothing. The double is annulled and the fencers stay where they are.     |

Because of the last row, at 14-14 you need a clean single touch. Throwing a hopeful counter-attack into the opponent's attack only gets you another 14-14 at best.

## (y-) The priority minute

If time runs out with the score tied, the referee draws lots to give one fencer priority. Then the fencers fence one more minute.

- The first valid single touch wins the bout.
- A double does not count. The score stays tied and fencing continues.
- If nobody scores in the minute, the fencer with priority wins.

The two fencers have opposite jobs in that minute. With priority, you can wait and defend, and the opponent has to come to you. Without it, you have one minute to score a clean touch against someone who is happy to do nothing.

## (y-) Non-combativity

About a minute of fencing without a touch counts as unwillingness to fight. The rule applies in direct elimination bouts, not in pools.

The FIE changed the penalty sequence at its 2025 Congress, and USA Fencing adopted the change on 1 October 2026. The old warning card (P-yellow) is gone. The first offense now gets a P-red card, which awards a touch against both fencers. The second gets a P-black, which ends the bout: the fencer with the lower score loses, and on a tie the fencer with the lower seeding loses.

In practice this means a careful, slow DE has less room than it used to. Both fencers score on the P-red, which works like a forced double. If you are behind, that hurts you the same way a double does.

## (y-) What it means for decisions

| Situation                          | Sensible approach                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Leading late in the third period   | Let the opponent come. Counter-attacks that risk doubles are acceptable.            |
| Trailing late in the third period  | Attack with clear tempo. Avoid trading. Use distance to prepare instead of rushing. |
| Priority minute, you have priority | Defend. Keep distance, parry, and avoid committing.                                 |
| Priority minute, no priority       | Build an attack with preparation early in the minute. Leave room for a second try.  |
| Long quiet stretch in a DE         | Expect a P-red soon. If you are behind, make something happen before it comes.      |
