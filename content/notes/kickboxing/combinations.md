The Dutch style of kickboxing is built on short punch combinations that finish with a kick, usually a low kick. The punches close the distance and bring the opponent's guard up, and the kick lands on the leg or body they've stopped watching. Finishing on a kick also leaves you at kicking range at the end, instead of standing in front of their hooks with nothing left to throw.

Numbers are punches, as on the [[notes/kickboxing/punches|(y-) Punches]] page: 1 jab, 2 cross, 3 lead hook, 4 rear hook, 5 and 6 uppercuts. All combinations are written for orthodox stance.

---

## (y-) Mental model

Each strike loads the next one through the hips. The cross drives your rear hip forward, which pulls the lead hip back and loads the lead leg for a switch kick. The lead hook turns the hips back the other way, which winds up the rear leg. So:

- **After a 2, the lead leg is loaded.** Follow with a switch kick or lead teep.
- **After a 3, the rear leg is loaded.** Follow with a rear low kick or rear body kick.

If a combination feels clumsy, check whether you're asking a leg to kick when the hips have just turned the wrong way for it.

The other principle is changing levels. Hit high to bring the hands up, then kick low, or kick low to bring the attention down, then go high.

## (y-) The core combinations

| Combination                                | Why it works                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1-2-rear low kick                          | The textbook Dutch combination. The 1-2 brings their hands up and their weight back onto the lead leg. |
| 1-2-3-rear low kick                        | The hook turns the hips and loads the rear leg. If a gym teaches one "Dutch combination", it's this.   |
| 1-2-switch kick (body)                     | The cross pulls the lead hip back, ready to swing. The kick lands on the open side of the body.        |
| 1-rear body kick                           | Short and fast. The jab covers the step out.                                                           |
| Rear low kick-2                            | After the kick lands, their weight shifts. The cross follows as your rear foot comes down.             |
| Teep-2                                     | The teep pushes them back. As they come forward again, they walk onto the cross.                       |
| 1-2-3 (body)-rear head kick                | The hook to the body brings their elbow down. The kick goes over it to the head.                       |
| Rear low kick-rear low kick-rear head kick | The same kick twice low, then the third time high, after their attention has dropped.                  |
| Check-2-rear low kick                      | The counter combination. Check their kick, then punch and kick while they reset.                       |
| 1-1-2-3-rear low kick                      | A longer version for pressure. Two jabs to walk them back to the ropes, then the standard finish.      |

Learn the first three before the others. Most of the rest are variations on them.

## (y-) How to drill a combination

1. **Shadowbox it slowly.** Five slow repetitions, checking that each strike turns the hips the right way for the next one.
2. **Pads at medium speed.** The holder calls it, you throw it, and you return to stance after the last strike.
3. **Add an exit.** After the kick, step out at an angle or back, guard up. Count the combination as finished only once you are back in stance and out of their range.
4. **Bag at full power.** Whole rounds of the same combination, with the last strike as hard as the first.
5. **Called at random.** The holder calls different combinations with no pattern. You react to the call.
6. **Sparring, one at a time.** Pick one combination per sparring round and try to land it.

## (y-) Building your own

- Start with one or two punches, finish with a kick.
- Check the hips: after a 2, kick with the lead leg. After a 3, kick with the rear leg.
- Change levels at least once.
- Keep it to four strikes or fewer. Longer combinations look good on pads and rarely land in sparring.

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                                            | Fix                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Punches with no weight behind them                 | Each punch has to be a real threat, or they won't bring their hands up to block it.           |
| Ending a combination on a punch, standing in front | Finish with a kick or step out. Standing still after the last punch is when you get hit back. |
| Standing still after the kick                      | Return to stance and move. The kick isn't the end of the exchange for them.                   |
| Dropping the hands to throw the kick               | The guard stays up through the whole combination.                                             |
| Kicking with the wrong leg for the hips            | Check the loading rule: 2 loads the lead leg, 3 loads the rear leg.                           |
| Combinations that are too long                     | Four strikes is plenty. Two good ones are better than six sloppy ones.                        |
