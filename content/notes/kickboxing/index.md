What I'm learning in kickboxing: punches, shin kicks, low kicks, and keeping my hands up, which taekwondo never asked of me.

---

Kickboxing is an umbrella name. It covers Dutch-style gyms, K-1 and Glory rules, amateur WAKO competition, and plenty of fitness classes that borrow the word. These notes follow the K-1 and Glory style: punches, kicks to the legs, body and head, knees, and no elbows. Muay Thai adds elbows and a full clinch, and it comes up wherever the two differ.

Coming from [[notes/taekwondo/index|(y) Taekwondo]], the kicks were the familiar part. The hands, a stance square enough to check a low kick, and a guard that stays up because the other person is allowed to punch my head were all new.

## (y-) Core concepts

| Concept         | Kickboxing specifics                                                                                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Stance          | More square than boxing or taekwondo, weight centered, so the lead leg can lift at any moment to check a kick.     |
| Guard           | High. Gloves at the cheeks, elbows down over the ribs, chin tucked. Head kicks are legal, so the hands don't drop. |
| Kicking surface | The shin, swung through the target. The foot only matters when the distance is long.                               |
| Legs as target  | Kicks to the thigh and calf are legal and score. Checking them is part of the basics.                              |
| Rhythm          | Punches set up kicks and kicks set up punches. Combinations usually end on a kick.                                 |
| Clinch          | Under Glory rules, grab only to throw one knee, then let go.                                                       |
| Rounds          | Three minutes in Glory, two minutes in amateur WAKO ring bouts, with a minute's rest between rounds.               |

## (y-) Fighters worth watching

| Fighter           | Country | What to watch for                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------- | ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ernesto Hoost     | NED     | Four-time K-1 World Grand Prix champion (1997, 1999, 2000, 2002), nicknamed "Mr. Perfect". Combinations that finish on a low kick.                                                              |
| Peter Aerts       | NED     | Three K-1 World Grand Prix titles (1994, 1995, 1998). Heavyweight high kicks.                                                                                                                   |
| Semmy Schilt      | NED     | Four K-1 World Grand Prix titles (2005, 2006, 2007, 2009) and the first Glory heavyweight champion in 2012. Very tall, and used the jab, teep and knees to keep people at the end of his reach. |
| Giorgio Petrosyan | ITA     | K-1 World MAX champion in 2009 and 2010. The model for defense: small steps, tight blocks, and a counter off every miss.                                                                        |
| Buakaw            | THA     | K-1 World MAX champion in 2004 and 2006, fighting as Buakaw Por. Pramuk. Muay Thai body kicks and knees under kickboxing rules.                                                                 |
| Rico Verhoeven    | NED     | Glory heavyweight champion from June 2014 until he gave up the title in November 2025, with thirteen defenses. Volume, pressure and low kicks.                                                  |
