Defense in kickboxing has to cover more ground than in boxing. Punches come at the head and body, kicks come at the head, body and legs, and knees come at whatever is lowest. Boxing-style head movement still works, but ducking low brings your head into knee and kick range, so kickboxers block more and slip less than boxers do.

---

## (y-) Mental model

Defense works in layers, from far to close:

| Layer          | What it does                                      | Example                                                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Distance       | Stay where their strikes fall short.              | Step back out of a jab, pull the lead leg away from a low kick. |
| Feet           | Change the angle so their attack misses the line. | Pivot off the line after they kick.                             |
| Legs           | Block or stop attacks below the waist.            | Check a low kick, teep to stop an advance.                      |
| Hands and arms | Block, parry or catch what reaches you.           | High guard against a hook, parry a jab.                         |
| Head           | Small movements, used sparingly.                  | Slip a cross to the outside.                                    |

Whatever you do, follow it with a counter. A block on its own just means you're getting hit somewhere less painful, and an opponent who never gets countered will keep attacking.

## (y-) The high guard (cover)

When something comes at your head and you can't get out of the way, cover it up.

1. Bring the glove on the side of the attack to your head, palm side against the temple, as if you were answering a phone.
2. Keep the elbow tight and pointing down so the forearm covers the side of the head and the elbow covers the ribs.
3. Brace the neck and tuck the chin. Keep your eyes open.
4. Turn slightly into the block, not away from it.

This is the defense against hooks and high roundhouses. Against a full-power head kick, the arm takes the force and your head moves with it, so step away from the kick while you cover if you can.

## (y-) Parry and catch

- **Parry the jab.** Your rear glove taps their jab down or across, a small movement right in front of your face. Don't reach out to meet it.
- **Catch the jab or cross.** Let it land in your rear glove, held against your cheek, and counter immediately with your own jab or cross.

## (y-) Slip and roll

- **Slip.** A small turn of the shoulders and a bend at the knees so the punch passes beside your head. Slip outside the cross (to your left, if you're orthodox and they are too) and you're in position for a lead hook.
- **Roll.** Bend the knees and move your head in a U-shape under a hook. Keep it shallow. A deep roll puts your head at knee and kick height.

## (y-) Body kicks

Against a rear roundhouse to your body, bring the arm on that side down, elbow toward the hip, and turn into it. Lifting the leg to meet the kick (the same as a check, but higher) adds another layer, as long as your hands stay up.

## (y-) Catching kicks

Catching a body kick is legal under Glory rules, but only briefly. You can grab the leg to throw a single strike immediately, with one step in any direction, and then you have to let go. Sweeps and throws are fouls. So catch it against your side with the arm on that side, hit them once (a cross is the usual choice), and release. In Muay Thai you could hold on and sweep the support leg, but that's illegal in Glory.

## (y-) Attack, defense, counter

Orthodox against orthodox.

| They throw           | You defend with                            | Then counter with                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Jab                  | Parry with the rear hand                   | Jab, or 1-2                              |
| Cross                | Slip outside, or catch on the rear glove   | Lead hook                                |
| Lead hook            | High guard with the rear arm               | Rear hook or cross                       |
| Rear body kick       | Arm down, elbow to hip, or lift the leg    | Cross, or your own rear kick             |
| Rear head kick       | High guard on that side, step away         | Low kick to the support leg as they land |
| Outside low kick     | [[notes/kickboxing/low-kicks\|(y-) Check]] | Cross, or rear low kick                  |
| Teep                 | Step off the line, or catch it             | One strike, then release                 |
| Pressure, walking in | Teep to the body                           | Low kick as they reset                   |

## (y-) Drills

| Drill               | How                                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One-for-one         | Partner throws one strike, you defend and counter with one strike. Swap after a round.               |
| Defense-only round  | Partner attacks lightly for a round. You aren't allowed to strike, only block, check, slip and move. |
| Guard under fatigue | Hold the high guard while a partner taps your gloves and arms for a full round. Hands never drop.    |
| Check and counter   | Partner throws light low kicks at random. Check every one and counter straight away.                 |
| Wall drill          | Back to the wall, partner throws light punches. Defend without moving your feet.                     |

## (y-) Common mistakes

| Mistake                           | Fix                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Blocking and freezing             | Always counter. Even a jab tells them attacking you costs something.                                   |
| Closing your eyes or turning away | Brace and watch. You can't counter something you didn't see end.                                       |
| Reaching out to parry             | Parry close to your face. Reaching opens the space the next punch goes through.                        |
| Ducking straight down             | Your head goes to knee height. Slip sideways and stay shallow.                                         |
| Elbows flaring in the high guard  | Elbows down and in. A flared elbow lets the body kick through.                                         |
| Backing straight up               | Step back at an angle. Retreating in a straight line just gives them the whole ring to chase you into. |
